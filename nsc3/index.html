Context

NSC3 is a real-time situational-awareness and secure-communications platform for control-room operators and mobile field personnel. It brings video, location information and communications into a shared operational picture, including in degraded network environments.

I work across the critical-communications portfolio, primarily as NSC3’s sole designer. I work with product management and developers to turn needs from customers, sales and support into clear requirements, then fit them into the existing product while keeping it coherent and usable.

Problem

Control-room operators needed to know when their sources captured something that required their attention. An AI detection or a body camera being activated, for example. The challenge was defining one understandable workflow for all these sources.

I defined these occurrences as an “events” concept. Then I needed to work out how to fit them into the product.

Process

I worked through the requirements with product management and developers, including what information operators needed and how events should relate to tasks. I considered using task chat as the event log, but chose a dedicated Events page so operators could inspect detections before deciding whether a response was needed.

I documented the flow and designed the interface in Figma. I reviewed the designs with product management and developers, whose questions helped resolve the remaining details before implementation. I also used the development version regularly to check how the implemented features behaved.

Field reports later showed that too many events were competing for operator attention. I used a detection-to-action diagram and a clickable low-fidelity prototype to work through the next iteration with the team. This led to personal subscriptions, notifications and changes to event inspection, which moved from above the log into an adjacent panel.

Solution

From a detection to a deliberate response.

Inspect and coordinate a response

The selected event opens beside the event log, keeping the surrounding activity visible while the operator examines the evidence and available actions.

From event inspection, operators can create a task to coordinate the response. I initially considered embedding task creation directly into the inspection screen. I decided to use the existing task creation modal instead, because saving one click didn’t justify changing a flow operators were using elsewhere in the product.

Operators can also send targeted alerts to chosen recipients. I designed the web and mobile experiences, including SMS and LiveLink delivery and acknowledgement tracking. The implementation also became the first practical pilot of the AI-assisted approach I later developed into a reusable workflow for rapid prototyping.

Manage competing demands for attention

Subscriptions let controllers define which events should notify them. In the image above, notification bells identify events that match those subscriptions. The “Notified events” filter lets operators narrow the event log to those matches.

Resolving a design debate: subscription placement

Design reviews often involve debating several possible solutions. With event subscriptions, I thought the controls belonged on the Events page: they were closely related to the events, and users would find them in context. The product director thought users would look under Manage, based on his experience and analysis of competing products.

We couldn’t test the alternatives with users at that point. I prepared designs for both options and brought their pros and cons to a team review. We discussed them and went with the product director’s preference.

The discussion was useful because it brought discoverability into focus. As I developed the Manage design, I paid particular attention to how the subscriptions were placed and labelled so users could find them and recognise them as their own.

Impact

From customer requests to key product capabilities.

Events and targeted alerting have become strategically important capabilities in NSC3’s offering and are now among its main selling points. Both have been implemented for customer and partner POCs and demo use.

On a later face-recognition POC, the on-site partner requested better ways to filter detections. I designed label search and confidence filtering within a day of receiving the requests. They were deployed within a week.
